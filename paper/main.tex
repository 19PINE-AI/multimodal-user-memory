\begin{abstract}\noindent
LM agents store user memory as text. Mem0, MemGPT, M3-Agent, and the wider conversational-memory line turn what the agent saw or heard into transcripts and captions, then retrieve over them. This works for \emph{captionable} content (``my cat is named Bibi'') but fails for \emph{perceptual} content: how a user's voice sounds, what their face looks like across lighting and age, what their kitchen sounds like, how tired they sound today relative to their baseline. Captioning these into text is a lossy projection that destroys the discriminative signal. Text-shaped memory is structurally incomplete for personalised multimodal agents.

We propose \textbf{parametric multimodal user memory}: per-modality banks of $(\text{encoder embedding}, \text{value embedding})$ rows attached to a frozen LM via a forward pre-hook on \texttt{lm\_head}. Insertion is a single tensor append ($\sim$0.5\,ms for 1000 identities); query latency is flat at $\sim$15\,ms regardless of bank size $N$. We construct \textbf{PerceptMem v0.2}, a five-sub-modality cross-condition benchmark: face identity (2180 IDs), painter style, speaker identity, acoustic scene, and paralinguistic state.

On PerceptMem, the mechanism exceeds the strongest text-free baseline (embedding-RAG cosine NN) on \emph{seven} multi-seed cells at $p{<}0.05$ --- it beats RAG because the bank's similarity is computed in the LM's value-side space, a different and sometimes more discriminative function than encoder cosine. The largest gains come from adversarial-aware pretraining, which converts encoder-confused regimes into surplus: \textbf{+71\,pp} on painter style, \textbf{+71\,pp} on paralinguistic state, +17\,pp on acoustic scene (perfect $1.000{\pm}0.000$ across all seeds), and +14\,pp on face cross-condition --- all $p{<}0.001$, $n{\ge}3$ seeds. The architecture transfers across Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen2.5-VL (Mistral-7B is recipe-sensitive). A clean structural finding from the VLM transfer: the bank's key encoder must be \emph{orthogonal} to the LM's value-side embedding space for the win to hold --- with external ArcFace as key, AttMem reaches 1.000 at $N{=}10$; with the VLM's native vision tokens (already in LM hidden space), the value-side prior adds no orthogonal signal --- AttMem only matches native-token cosine at $N{=}10$ and trails it at larger $N$, and stays far below the ArcFace ceiling throughout.

A discrete-codebook predecessor we tuned over 16 development cycles caps at $\sim$7\% retr@1 at $N{\ge}300$ regardless of codebook size, encoder, or compute --- the quantisation step itself is the binding constraint, a learned analog of text-captioning's information loss. The mechanism is $52{\times}$ faster than RAG-with-LM-context at $N{=}1000$; RAG OOMs at $N{=}10000$. Text recall is preserved byte-for-byte; the parametric memory composes \emph{additively} with text memory. Code: \url{https://github.com/bojieli/multimodal-user-memory}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

When an LM agent ``remembers'' something about its user, what does it actually store? In the dominant paradigm, it stores \emph{text}. Mem0~\cite{mem0}, MemoryLLM~\cite{memllm}, MemGPT / Letta~\cite{memgpt,letta}, M3-Agent~\cite{m3agent}, and the wider conversational-memory line --- evaluated through benchmarks like LongMemEval~\cite{longmemeval} --- turn what the agent saw or heard into transcripts (via ASR~\cite{whisper}) and captions (via BLIP-2~\cite{blip2} or LLaVA~\cite{llava}), push those fragments into a sentence-encoder vector store~\cite{sbert}, and retrieve at inference. Personalised vision-language work (MyVLM~\cite{myvlm}, Yo'LLaVA~\cite{yollava}, MC-LLaVA~\cite{mcllava}, Online-PVLM~\cite{onlinepvlm}, RAP~\cite{rap}, TAME~\cite{tame}) retains textual addressing implicitly: a \emph{named} concept (``Bibi'') is the identifier; the visual content is auxiliary.

This works for the easy slice of user memory --- the part that is \emph{captionable}. ``My cat is named Bibi.'' ``My favourite restaurant is in Paris.'' ``I am vegetarian.'' These propositions survive the journey through text intact. But the easy slice is not all of user memory. Consider what an attentive companion actually remembers about a person:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
  \item How their voice sounds when they say their own name --- and how that differs from how a stranger says it.
  \item How their face looks today --- subtly older than last year's photo, but unmistakably them.
  \item How their brushwork looks --- ``this is by the same painter as the one Tuesday, even though it's from a different period.''
  \item What their kitchen sounds like --- ``you're calling from the same room as yesterday.''
  \item How tired they sound today --- relative to their baseline last week.
\end{itemize}

None of this is captionable in any useful sense. ``A person with brown hair'' does not let you tell two people apart. ``The user sounded tired'' cannot distinguish today's tired from last week's tired. The moment we project these characteristics into text, we destroy the discriminative signal. \textbf{Text-shaped memory is a lossy channel for perceptual user content, and the loss is fundamental to the modality.}

The structural claim: \emph{captionable} and \emph{perceptual} user content require different memory primitives. ``My cat is named Bibi'' belongs in a text vector store; \emph{how Bibi looks across lighting and angle} does not. The latter needs a primitive that stores perceptual content in its native modality.

\paragraph{Our proposal.} \textbf{Parametric multimodal user memory (PMUM):} per-modality banks of (encoder embedding, value embedding) rows bolted onto a frozen LM via a forward pre-hook on \texttt{lm\_head}. Registration is a single tensor append (no SGD at the per-user level); query is cross-attention over the bank, fused into the LM as a residual at the logit-injection point. The result is content-addressable memory in the LM's value-side embedding space, with no captioning intermediate.

\paragraph{Why this matters now.} Multimodal personalisation is moving fast~\cite{onlinepvlm,rap}, but the user-memory layer has not caught up. Existing systems either offload perceptual identity to a face/speaker recognition tool that returns a text label (M3-Agent~\cite{m3agent}, back to lossy-text-space), or train a per-concept adapter (LoRA per concept~\cite{lora}, MyVLM's per-concept head~\cite{myvlm}, Yo'LLaVA's per-concept tokens~\cite{yollava}), which does not compose to user-scale memory of dozens of perceptual characteristics. What is missing is a single content-addressable parametric primitive for perceptual user content. Our mechanism takes the spirit of kNN-LM~\cite{khandelwal2020nearest} and Memorizing Transformers~\cite{wu2022memorizing} --- attention over a non-parametric memory bank --- and specialises it to per-modality user-content registration with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ insertion.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Framing.} We articulate why text-shaped memory is structurally incomplete for perceptual user content, and why a parametric multimodal memory is the right primitive for the missing slice.
\item \textbf{Mechanism.} A bolt-on continuous-attention memory over per-modality (encoder, value) banks. $\sim$8M trainable parameters over a 3.1B frozen LM; $\mathcal{O}(1)$ insertion; flat $\sim$15\,ms query regardless of $N$. Four critical bug-fixes separate a working implementation from three earlier attempts that produced random output (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Benchmark.} \textbf{PerceptMem v0.2}, five cross-condition sub-modalities chosen because text-based memory provably cannot carry the discriminating signal: face identity (2180 IDs), painter style, speaker identity, acoustic scene, paralinguistic state.
\item \textbf{Empirical claim.} The mechanism exceeds the embedding-RAG cosine-NN ceiling on seven sub-modality$\times$$N$ cells at $p{<}0.05$ (multi-seed), including face identity at the 2180-ID scale ($p{=}0.006$, $n{=}4$). Across all five sub-modalities, it reaches 83--117\% of the encoder ceiling. A discrete-codebook predecessor we tuned for 16 development cycles caps at 7\% retr@1 at $N{\ge}300$ regardless of codebook size or compute --- a $\sim$9$\times$ gap at scale (Fig.~\ref{fig:pivot}).
\item \textbf{Honest map.} An ablation showing exactly when the parametric mechanism beats cosine, when it merely matches, and when training the bolt-on parameters \emph{hurts} (encoder already perfect; \S\ref{sec:training_matters}). The answer to ``what does it add beyond cosine?'' is: discriminative signal from the LM's value-side embedding, in the regime where the encoder's cosine is imperfect.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why text memory cannot solve cross-condition perceptual recall}\label{sec:why}

Consider speaker-identity recall under cross-recording conditions (the A-XR-ID sub-modality, drawing from LibriSpeech~\cite{librispeech}). A user has 10 voice samples on file from prior sessions; a new sample arrives. Which of the 10 is it?

\textbf{Text RAG} (Mem0~\cite{mem0}, MemoryLLM~\cite{memllm}). Caption each voice into text (e.g.\ Whisper~\cite{whisper} transcript plus a speaker descriptor): ``A male voice, mid-30s, slight Eastern-European accent.'' Embed with a sentence encoder~\cite{sbert}; cosine-retrieve. \emph{This will almost always fail.} The caption applies to thousands of speakers; cosine cannot distinguish 10 registered users with even 50\% accuracy. The signal that uniquely identifies a speaker --- voice timbre, glottal pulse shape, prosodic micro-patterns --- is destroyed in the text projection.

\textbf{Tool call} (M3-Agent~\cite{m3agent}). Call a face/speaker recognition tool (ArcFace~\cite{arcface}, ECAPA-TDNN~\cite{ecapa}), receive a text label (``speaker 47''), store that. The user's memory of speaker 47 is now \emph{just a text token}: the perceptual fingerprint is never integrated into the LM's representation. At inference the LM can recall that the label was assigned, but cannot reason about how the voice sounded.

\textbf{Embedding RAG.} Skip the captioning step: index the raw encoder embedding (ECAPA-TDNN for speakers, ArcFace for faces, CLIP~\cite{clip} for style); cosine-retrieve at inference. This is the strongest text-free baseline and the one we treat seriously. It succeeds when the encoder is perfectly cross-condition invariant and fails when it is not. RAG cosine NN reaches 0.93 retr@1 at $N{=}10$ on 2180-ID face cross-condition --- non-trivial but not at ceiling.

\textbf{Per-concept gradient training} (Yo'LLaVA~\cite{yollava}, MyVLM~\cite{myvlm}). Train a per-concept token or adapter via SGD on registration samples. Insertion cost is $\sim$1\,s per identity; the resulting memory is concept-specific, not modality-general.

\textbf{Parametric multimodal user memory (this paper).} Store the encoder embedding as key and the LM's value-side embedding (for an assigned marker token) as value. At inference, cross-attention over the bank produces a residual injected at the \texttt{lm\_head} pre-forward, biasing the next-token logit toward the matching marker. The LM gets a content-addressable memory in its own representation space, with no captioning intermediate. The mechanism specialises kNN-LM~\cite{khandelwal2020nearest} and Memorizing Transformers~\cite{wu2022memorizing} --- attention over a non-parametric bank --- to per-modality \emph{user content} (not text continuations), with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ insertion.

\section{Method: Parametric Multimodal User Memory}\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{figs/fig0_arch.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Architecture.} A frozen pretrained LM (Qwen2.5-3B) is augmented by a forward pre-hook on \texttt{lm\_head}. Per-modality continuous-attention banks store $(\text{key}{=}\text{encoder embedding}, \text{value}{=}\text{LM value-side embedding for a marker token})$; at the hook, cross-attention over the bank produces a residual added to the post-norm hidden state. Insertion is a single tensor append. Total $\sim$8M trainable parameters on top of 3.1B frozen.}
  \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

The bank stores $(\bm{k}_i, \bm{v}_i)$ pairs where $\bm{k}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is the L2-normalised encoder embedding of registered sample $i$ and $\bm{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^H$ is the LM's value-side embedding for an assigned marker token (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). For tied input/output embeddings (Qwen2.5-3B), $\bm{v}_i = \texttt{input\_embedding}[m_i]$; for untied models (Qwen2.5-7B), $\bm{v}_i = \texttt{lm\_head.weight}[m_i]$ (auto-detected). The residual addition pre-\texttt{lm\_head} then yields a clean per-marker logit boost $\texttt{lm\_head}[m_i]\!\cdot\!\bm{v}_i = \|\bm{v}_i\|^2$, rather than a cross-product of two unrelated learned vectors.

At the attached LM layer, for each perceptual position with hidden state $\bm{h}$ and encoder-derived query $\bm{q}$ (L2-normalised),
\begin{equation}
\bm{w} = \mathrm{softmax}\bigl(\beta \, \bm{q}^\top \bm{K}\bigr)\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^N,\quad
\bm{r} = \bm{w}^\top \bm{V}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^H,\quad
\bm{h}' = \bm{h} + g \cdot W_o \, \bm{r}
\end{equation}
where $\bm{K}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$, $\bm{V}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{N \times H}$, $\beta = e^{\log\beta}$ is a learned inverse temperature, $g$ is a learned scalar gain, and $W_o \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times H}$ is a learned output projection (initialised at $I$). Total trainable parameters $\sim$8M.

\paragraph{Four critical bug-fixes.} The architecture above is the \emph{fourth} iteration. Three earlier attempts produced random-level retr@1 ($\sim$0.07 at $N{=}5$) despite plausible-looking loss curves. The four bug fixes:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
  \item \textbf{No $\sqrt{D}$ divisor.} With L2-normalised keys, dividing softmax logits by $\sqrt{D}$ shrinks cosine-difference logits below 0.1 for $D{\ge}512$, yielding near-uniform attention.
  \item \textbf{$\log\beta$ init $= \log 20$.} Sharp attention at zero-shot.
  \item \textbf{Hook on \texttt{lm\_head} pre-forward}, not on \texttt{model.layers[K]}: the residual reaches the logit without dilution through later transformer blocks and the final norm.
  \item \textbf{$g{=}8.0$ (learnable).} The natural boost $\bm{v}_i{\cdot}\bm{v}_i \approx 1.2$ is dwarfed by the LM's negative logit prior for unusual marker tokens; the gain scales the residual to dominate.
\end{itemize}
A fifth, scaling-only choice --- \emph{curriculum bank size} --- is not a correctness fix but matters at large $N$ (\S\ref{sec:results}, Fig.~\ref{fig:ablations}b).

\paragraph{Pretraining.} Each step samples $bs \sim \text{Uniform}[bs_{\min}, bs_{\max}]$ (e.g.\ $[64, 1024]$ for V-XC-ID-XXXL), draws $bs$ training identities, inserts $(\bm{k}, m)$ pairs, samples a cross-condition query, and minimises cross-entropy on the next-token logit at the perceptual position. 5K--12K steps suffice for all five sub-modalities (vs Path~A's 100K).

PerceptMem evaluates \emph{memory}, not perception: each sub-modality has a fixed encoder treated as black-box infrastructure that any baseline can use. The API is \texttt{register} and \texttt{recall}. Data is cross-session; identities are partitioned into train and eval pools so pretraining sees no overlap with evaluated identities. For each $N$, we register $N$ distinct identities (one sample each), then issue 3 cross-condition queries per identity, with argmax restricted to the registered markers. \textbf{Embedding RAG} (same encoder, cosine NN over registered keys) is the strongest text-free baseline and our reference to beat. We call it the \emph{encoder ceiling} because it is the best one can do with encoder-cosine similarity; it is not a hard ceiling for AttMem, which computes similarity in the LM's value-side space --- a different and sometimes more discriminative function (\S\ref{sec:scorecard}).

\subsection{Why captioning intermediates fail: the design-space finding}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{figs/fig6_pivot.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Discrete codebook $\to$ continuous attention.} Replacing the codebook with continuous attention over the (key, value) bank yields 2--10$\times$ retr@1 per sub-modality. A discrete codebook is a quantisation step that loses the perceptual signal the encoder preserved --- a learned analog of text captioning's information loss.}
  \label{fig:pivot}
\end{figure}

We tuned Path~A to its ceiling over 16 development cycles. Increasing $K$ from 32 to 1024 lifts the same-code rate from 0.32 to 0.53 but causes gate retrieval to collapse from 0.51 to 0.16; net retr@1 is unchanged across the $K$ sweep. Swapping the face encoder to AntelopeV2 R100 (trained on 360K identities) gives the same same-code rate. 100K-step continual pretraining on the expanded 2180-ID pool moves no needles.

\textbf{The quantisation step itself is the binding constraint.} Two encoder embeddings in the same codebook cell are indistinguishable downstream; at $N{\ge}300$ the collision rate dominates retr@1. This is a learned analog of the text-captioning information loss from \S\ref{sec:why}: any intermediate categorical representation discards the perceptual signal the encoder worked to preserve. Continuous attention over the raw embedding sidesteps the quantisation entirely.

  \label{fig:latency}
\end{figure}

Query latency is flat at $\sim$15\,ms regardless of $N$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}); the bank matmul is microseconds. Batch insertion is constant $\sim$0.5\,ms (one \texttt{torch.cat}); per-id cost shrinks from 0.025\,ms at $N{=}10$ to 0.0001\,ms at $N{=}10000$. \textbf{RAG-with-LM-context} (prepending registered keys as text tokens) grows linearly per query and architecturally OOMs beyond Qwen's 32k context window. Our mechanism is 52$\times$ faster at $N{=}1000$ and the only of the two that operates at $N{=}10000$. Against Path~A's 80-step SGD per id, batch insertion of 1000 ids is $\sim$2{,}000{,}000$\times$ faster.

\subsection{Training matters: when the parametric memory adds value over cosine}\label{sec:training_matters}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{figs/fig3_training_matters.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Effect of pretraining}, per sub-modality, as $\Delta$ retr@1 from zero-shot to trained. Three regimes: (i) training beats embedding-RAG at scale on V-XC-ID (navy; $+0.40$ at $N{=}700$); (ii) training hurts on A-XR-ID (red; encoder ceiling already 1.00, $W_o$ adds noise); (iii) encoder $+$ $k$NN-LM alone beats cosine at small $N$ on low-ceiling encoders (V-STY $N{=}5$).}
  \label{fig:training}
\end{figure}

To disambiguate ``what does the parametric memory add beyond cosine?'', we evaluate at $n_{\text{steps}}{=}0$ (every parameter at initialisation: $W_o{=}I$, $g{=}8$, $\log\beta{=}\log 20$) and compare to the trained model. Three regimes (Fig.~\ref{fig:training}):

\textbf{(i) Training drives the win at scale.} On V-XC-ID-XXXL, zero-shot is below RAG at every $N$; trained beats RAG at $N{=}10$. The lift from training grows from $+0.13$ at $N{=}10$ to $+0.40$ at $N{=}700$. Pretraining produces real per-modality discriminative power, not just inherited cosine.

\textbf{(ii) Training hurts when the encoder is already perfect.} On A-XR-ID (RAG $=1.00$), zero-shot matches the ceiling exactly (1.000 at $N{\in}\{5, 10\}$); trained $W_o$/$g$ adds noise that costs $\sim$0.10. When cosine NN is at 1.00, the LM has nothing useful to add.

\textbf{(iii) Encoder $+$ $k$NN-LM alone can beat cosine.} On V-STY $N{=}5$, zero-shot reaches 0.533 vs RAG 0.400. The bank's structural mechanism (soft histogram over value-side embeddings) suffices at small $N$ when the encoder is low-ceiling.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{figs/fig5_ablations.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Ablations on V-XC-ID-XXXL.} \textbf{(a)} Within fixed compute, larger LM is not better: Qwen2.5-3B at 50K steps wins at $N{\ge}50$; Qwen2.5-7B (untied embeddings, our \texttt{lm\_head.weight} value fix) beats 3B at $N{\le}20$ but lags at scale within the same step budget. \textbf{(b)} Curriculum bank size $bs{\sim}\text{Uniform}[64,1024]$ closes the train/eval distribution shift that fixed $bs{=}64$ training suffers at $N{\ge}100$ ($+0.43$ retr@1 at $N{=}700$).}
  \label{fig:ablations}
\end{figure}

\textbf{LM size $\times$ steps} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablations}a). Qwen2.5-7B (untied embeddings, 28 layers, hidden 3584) beats Qwen2.5-3B at small $N$ but lags at large $N$ within 12K steps. At 50K steps the gap narrows but does not close (3B@50K at $N{=}1000$: 0.625; 7B@50K: 0.569). The tied-vs-untied embedding distinction is load-bearing in both Qwen-7B and Llama-3.1-8B: the bank value must be \texttt{lm\_head.weight[marker]}, not \texttt{input\_embedding[marker]}.

\textbf{Curriculum bank size} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablations}b). Fixed $bs{=}64$ training causes a train/eval distribution shift at large $N$: at $N{=}700$, retr@1 drops from 0.63 (curriculum) to 0.20 (fixed). Curriculum bank-size sampling is essential for the scaling story.

\subsection{Adversarial regime: standard training fails, adv-training transforms}\label{sec:adversarial}

The wins in §\ref{sec:scorecard} use \emph{random} bank composition. We additionally evaluate against \emph{adversarial} distractors: for each query, the bank contains the target plus the top-$K$ most-cosine-similar non-matching identities (Fig.~\ref{fig:adversarial}a; Qwen-3B). This is the hardest retrieval setting --- by construction, encoder cosine NN is confused.

\textbf{Standard-trained AttMem loses to RAG on adversarial.} With $\texttt{adv\_prob}{=}0$ on Qwen2.5-3B, AttMem trails RAG cosine NN by 2--16\,pp at $K{=}19$ on three of four sub-modalities where the encoder has headroom (Table~\ref{tab:adv_std}). The encoder-confused regime leaves no room for the LM's value-side prior to add orthogonal signal.

\subsection{Cross-family generalisation: Qwen 3B/7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B}\label{sec:cross_family}

The architecture transfers across LM families with no per-model tuning beyond the auto-detected tied/untied value fix.

\textbf{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} (untied, 32 layers, hidden 4096) trained with our recipe beats RAG at $N{=}5$ and $N{=}10$ (both 1.000 vs RAG 0.933) and \emph{outperforms} Qwen-3B at large $N$ (0.676 vs 0.629 at $N{=}700$; 0.622 vs 0.594 at $N{=}1000$). On adversarial banks at $K{=}19$, Llama-3.1-8B matches or beats RAG at $K \ge 5$ even with \emph{standard} training (Fig.~\ref{fig:adversarial}b): the larger LM's richer value-side embedding space adds discriminative signal where Qwen-3B fails. Combined with adv-training, Llama+$\texttt{adv\_prob}{=}0.3$ matches Qwen-3B+adv on adversarial ($K{=}19$ both 0.986, $+14.5$\,pp over RAG) but preserves better random performance ($N{=}10$: 0.90 vs Qwen-3B+adv's 0.83) --- the recommended configuration when both regimes matter.

\textbf{Cross-family is not always free at fixed compute.} Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 at the same budget (12K steps, default $\texttt{lr}{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$) does \emph{not} reach Qwen/Llama-level convergence (final loss 6.20 vs Qwen-3B's 3.35) and underperforms on V-XC-ID-XXXL ($N{=}10$: 0.77; adversarial $K{=}19$: 0.80, $3.7$\,pp below RAG). The recipe transfers Qwen 3B$\to$7B and Qwen$\to$Llama-3.1-8B; Mistral likely needs a smaller learning rate or longer training.

\subsection{Cross-modal independence and text-recall non-regression}\label{sec:nonregression}

We register 20 face IDs and 15 speaker IDs in the same model and let argmax span the union of markers; cross-modal leak is 0.017 (vision queries) and 0.067 (audio queries), \emph{zero-shot} --- without per-modality training the banks are already independent. On 8 propositional English prompts, top-1 next-token prediction is byte-identical to vanilla Qwen across all configurations (hook-no-op, bolt with empty bank, bolt with populated banks). The hook itself is byte-perfect; propositional and perceptual memory primitives compose cleanly.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What parametric multimodal user memory is and isn't.} The claim is structural: text-shaped memory is the wrong primitive for perceptual user content, and a parametric multimodal memory is the right one. The empirical wins show the mechanism extracts more discriminative signal than embedding-RAG cosine NN over the same registered keys --- the LM's value-side embedding adds power orthogonal to encoder cosine. The two similarity functions (cosine on encoder vs cosine on value-side) are simply different. The win does not appear on clean-encoder sub-modalities (A-XR-ID at RAG=1.00) where there is no headroom; it appears where encoder cosine is imperfect (face cross-condition at scale; style with mid-layer CLIP).

\paragraph{Where the LM's value-side prior actually matters.} Three regimes (Table~\ref{tab:advtrain}, Fig.~\ref{fig:training}): \textbf{(i) random bank, imperfect encoder} — standard training beats RAG by extracting LM-side signal orthogonal to encoder cosine; \textbf{(ii) adversarial bank, imperfect encoder} — only adv-training puts the bank in the LM's ``natural'' attention regime, lifting retrieval by up to 71\,pp; \textbf{(iii) clean encoder} — no headroom, AttMem matches RAG. \emph{Value is measurable exactly where cosine NN over the encoder is imperfect on the deployment population.}

\paragraph{Why scaling the frozen LM doesn't trivially help.} Within 12K steps, a larger LM is worse at large $N$ in the random regime; at 50K steps the gap narrows but does not close. The bottleneck at large $N$ is the encoder's cross-condition discriminability and the gradient signal at the bank's softmax, neither of which scales with LM capacity at this regime. Larger LM \emph{does} matter on the adversarial regime --- a richer value-side embedding space is what lets adv-training retain better random performance (Llama-3.1-8B+adv preserves 0.90 vs Qwen-3B+adv's 0.83 at $N{=}10$).

\paragraph{Composition with text memory.} The parametric multimodal memory is \emph{additive} to existing text memory~\cite{mem0,memllm,memgpt,letta}, not a replacement. Captionable content (``my favourite restaurant is in Paris'') belongs in a text store and is reliably retrieved by sentence-encoder cosine~\cite{sbert}. Perceptual content (``how Bibi looks across lighting and age'') belongs in the parametric memory. The two compose: text recall is preserved byte-for-byte (\S\ref{sec:nonregression}); the perceptual memory fires only at perceptual positions. An agent with both primitives strictly dominates one with only text memory. As benchmarks like LongMemEval~\cite{longmemeval} extend to perceptual content, the gap will be quantifiable directly.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i) \textbf{Encoder ceiling at large $N$}: AttMem reaches 0.59 at $N{=}1000$ vs the encoder's 0.77 ceiling; further compute gives diminishing returns. (ii) \textbf{Scale beyond 2180 IDs not yet measured} with real cross-condition data (the latency benchmark to $N{=}10{,}000$ confirms architectural feasibility). (iii) \textbf{VLM with native vision tokens only matches native-token cosine at small $N$ and trails it at scale} (and is far below the ArcFace ceiling throughout); an external task-specific encoder is preferred (Table~\ref{tab:vlm}). (iv) \textbf{Cross-family is not free at fixed compute}: Mistral-7B did not converge in our budget. (v) \textbf{Online-PVLM}~\cite{onlinepvlm}, the closest prior, has not released code or checkpoints; a head-to-head must wait.

\section{Conclusion}

LM agents need a memory primitive that handles perceptual user content directly, without a captioning intermediate. The argument is structural: for the same reason text RAG works on captionable content (sentence-encoder cosine over text is informative), a parametric multimodal memory is the right primitive for perceptual content (encoder cosine over face/voice/style is informative once you commit to keep the content in its native modality). The two primitives are complementary; an agent equipped with both strictly dominates one with only text memory.

Empirically, the mechanism beats embedding-RAG cosine NN on seven multi-seed cells across two complementary regimes: standard training on random banks (up to $+24$\,pp on V-STY) and adversarial-aware training on encoder-confused banks ($+14$ to $+71$\,pp across four of five sub-modalities, $p{<}0.001$). The architecture transfers across Qwen 2.5 3B/7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen 2.5-VL --- with a clean architectural finding from the VLM transfer: the bank's key space must be orthogonal to the LM's value-side space for the win to hold, so the framework prefers an external, modality-specific encoder over a VLM's general-purpose vision tokens. Text recall is preserved byte-for-byte (\S\ref{sec:nonregression}); the parametric memory composes \emph{additively} with existing text-shaped agent memory.

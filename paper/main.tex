\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Personalised multimodal memory has converged on a single regime: a \emph{nameable} visual concept is registered once and recalled later in similar conditions. The harder case --- \emph{cross-condition} perceptual recall (same person under different lighting, same voice across recordings, same painter across periods, same acoustic scene across takes, same user's paralinguistic state) --- is unsolved. We propose \textbf{Perceptual Engram}, a bolt-on continuous attention memory attached to a frozen pretrained language model via a forward pre-hook on \texttt{lm\_head}, with three load-bearing ingredients: invariance-preserving perceptual encoders, a per-modality bank of (encoder embedding, marker input embedding) pairs, and pretrained $k$NN-LM-style logit injection trained for $\le 12$K steps with $\sim$8M trainable parameters. We construct \textbf{PerceptMem~v0.2}, a unified scorecard across five sub-modalities. At V-XC-ID $N{=}10$ on a 2180-ID face pool, our purely parametric retrieval (0.989 $\pm$ 0.014, $n{=}3$ seeds) \beats{} the embedding-RAG cosine-NN ceiling (0.933) with $p{=}0.038$ (extending to $p{=}0.006$ at $n{=}4$), and BEATS additionally at V-STY $N{=}5$ ($p{=}0.015$, 1.6$\times$ ratio) and V-STY $N{=}10$ ($p{=}0.009$). Across all five sub-modalities AttMem reaches 83--117\% of the encoder ceiling, while a discrete-codebook predecessor (\patha{}) capped at $\sim$7\% retr@1 at $N\ge 300$ regardless of codebook size, encoder upgrade, or 100K-step continual pretraining. Insertion is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ wall-clock ($\sim$0.5 ms for 1000 IDs combined) --- 2,000,000$\times$ faster than the SGD-per-id baseline. Query latency is flat at 15 ms over $N$ from 10 to 10000; RAG-with-LM-context is 52$\times$ slower at $N{=}1000$ and OOMs at $N{=}10000$ within Qwen's 32k context window. Top-1 next-token prediction is preserved byte-for-byte on text-only inputs, satisfying the no-regression-on-text-recall constraint. Code, results, and the PerceptMem benchmark are released at \url{https://github.com/bojieli/multimodal-user-memory}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The personalisation literature for multimodal large language models has converged on a recognisable regime. A user registers a small set of nameable visual concepts---\emph{this is Bibi}, \emph{this is my office mug}---and the system later answers questions or generates captions that reference those concepts. The recent wave of work in this regime (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, MC-LLaVA, Online-PVLM, RAP, TAME)~\cite{myvlm,yollava,mcllava,onlinepvlm,rap,tame} and the benchmarks that score it (Mem-Gallery, LCMP, MemoryCD)~\cite{memgallery,lcmp,memorycd} implicitly assume that the concept is \emph{named}, that the registration condition and the recall condition are \emph{perceptually similar}, and that the memory's job is essentially a lookup keyed by a stable label.

This regime is the easy slice of perceptual memory. The hard slice---and the one that matches how humans actually use perceptual memory---is content that \emph{resists naming as a concept}: paralinguistic prosody of a known speaker, style and authorship, acoustic scene, cross-condition perceptual reappearance under different lighting / angle / age / microphone. None of the existing systems address this hard slice, and no existing benchmark probes it. Published methods are either \emph{vision-only} (every system above), or extract a textual entity label and offload the heavy lifting to a separate face/speaker recognition tool, or treat memory as a special-token vocabulary extension trained per concept. None admit a \emph{continuous} perceptual memory keyed by an invariance-preserving encoder.

The natural mechanism for continuous perceptual memory is a content-addressable parametric module: register a percept by writing its encoder embedding into a per-user table, recall by querying the table with a different sample of the same identity. This is the design we explore. We make three claims about the resulting system, all of which we verify empirically:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Continuous attention memory beats discrete-codebook parametric memory} by 2--10$\times$ retr@1 across all five sub-modalities tested. We show this by direct comparison against a discrete-codebook predecessor (\patha{}) that we built and tuned to its ceiling over 16 sessions before pivoting.
  \item \textbf{The mechanism, in 3 of 5 sub-modality $\times$ $N$ cells we test, \beats{} the embedding-RAG cosine-NN ceiling}, multi-seed verified at $p{<}0.05$. This includes a face-identity task at $N{=}10$ over a 2180-ID pool, the largest scale at which any parametric perceptual memory has been shown to beat embedding RAG.
  \item \textbf{The system is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ at insertion and effectively $\mathcal{O}(1)$ at query} in wall-clock terms. Registration of 1000 identities takes 0.5\,ms (a single tensor concatenation); query latency is dominated by the LM forward ($\sim$15\,ms) and is flat over $N$ from 10 to 10000.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the architecture, including the four design choices that distinguish the working implementation from three earlier attempts that produced random output. Section~\ref{sec:bench} constructs PerceptMem~v0.2. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the full empirical evaluation: the BEATS-RAG cells, the \patha{}~$\to$~\attmem{} improvement, the latency benchmark, the training-matters disambiguation, and the no-text-regression validation. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the design space and limitations.

\section{Related work and positioning}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Per-user nameable visual concept identity.} MyVLM~\cite{myvlm} trains a per-concept binary classifier head plus a concept embedding in feature space. Yo'LLaVA~\cite{yollava} uses a special-token approach ($\sim$16 tokens per concept). MC-LLaVA~\cite{mcllava} adds multi-concept inference. RAP~\cite{rap} introduces a K-V retrieval database. Online-PVLM~\cite{onlinepvlm} provides train-free closed-form visual concept insertion at scale (OP-Eval at 1{,}292 concepts) via frozen Omni Concept Embedder $\to$ instance norm $\to$ mean pool $\to$ MLP project, with a concept memory bank for cross-session recall. All five are \emph{vision-only} and target \emph{nameable concept identity} under near-identical conditions.

\paragraph{Multimodal memory benchmarks.} Mem-Gallery~\cite{memgallery} (240 conversations, 1,003 images) explicitly excludes audio and does not test perceptual variation across sessions. LCMP+TAME~\cite{lcmp,tame} tests \emph{attribute} updates (``the dog had a haircut'') on 30 GPT-Image-1-generated concepts---attribute variation, not perceptual variation. None of these benchmarks probes \emph{cross-condition perceptual variation}.

\paragraph{Our positioning.} Existing personalised multimodal memory works for nameable visual concept identity under near-identical conditions; we extend it to audio entirely, to non-identity perceptual qualities (style, prosody, scene) and cross-condition reappearance via a single mechanism. We are the first to demonstrate multi-seed-verified parametric-beats-RAG at the 2180-ID scale on cross-condition face identity.

\section{Method: Continuous Attention Memory}\label{sec:method}

The bank stores $(\bm{k}_i, \bm{v}_i)$ pairs where $\bm{k}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is the L2-normalised encoder embedding of registered sample $i$ and $\bm{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^H$ is the LM's value-side embedding for an assigned marker token. For models with tied input/output embeddings (Qwen2.5-3B), $\bm{v}_i = \texttt{input\_embedding}[m_i]$; for untied models (Qwen2.5-7B), $\bm{v}_i = \texttt{lm\_head.weight}[m_i]$ (auto-detected). This choice ensures the residual addition pre-\texttt{lm\_head} produces a clean per-marker logit boost via $\texttt{lm\_head}[m_i] \cdot \bm{v}_i = \|\cdot\|^2$, rather than a cross-product of two unrelated learned vectors.

At the attached LM layer, for each perceptual position with hidden state $\bm{h}$ and encoder-derived query $\bm{q}$ (L2-normalised), the hook computes:
\begin{align}
\bm{w}      &= \mathrm{softmax}\big(\beta \cdot \bm{q}^\top \bm{K}\big) \in \mathbb{R}^N, &
\bm{r}      &= \bm{w}^\top \bm{V} \in \mathbb{R}^H, \\
\Delta \bm{h} &= g \cdot W_o \, \bm{r}, &
\bm{h}'     &= \bm{h} + \Delta \bm{h}.
\end{align}
where $\bm{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$, $\bm{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times H}$, $\beta = \exp(\log\beta)$ is a learned inverse temperature, $g$ is a learned scalar gain, and $W_o \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times H}$ is a learned output projection (initialised at identity). Total trainable parameters: $\sim$8M (on top of 3.1B frozen LM).

\paragraph{The four critical design choices.} The architecture above is the \emph{fourth} iteration. Three earlier attempts produced random-level retr@1 (0.07 at $N{=}5$) despite plausible-looking loss curves. The four bug fixes:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{No $\sqrt{D}$ divisor.} With L2-normalised keys, dividing softmax logits by $\sqrt{D}$ shrinks cosine-difference logits below 0.1 for $D \ge 512$, producing near-uniform attention.
  \item \textbf{$\log\beta$ init $= \log 20$.} Sharp attention at zero-shot.
  \item \textbf{Hook on \texttt{lm\_head} pre-forward}, not on \texttt{model.layers[K]}. The residual reaches logits without dilution through additional transformer blocks and the final norm.
  \item \textbf{$g = 8.0$ (learnable).} The natural logit boost from $\bm{v}_i \cdot \bm{v}_i \approx \|\bm{v}\|^2 \approx 1.2$ is dwarfed by the LM's natural-negative logit for unusual marker tokens ($-10$ to $-20$); the gain scales the residual to dominate.
\end{itemize}
A fifth design choice---\emph{curriculum bank size}---is needed only at large $N$ (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Pretraining recipe.} Each step samples bank size $bs \sim \text{Uniform}[bs_\min, bs_\max]$ (e.g.~$[64, 1024]$ for V-XC-ID-XXXL), draws $bs$ training identities, inserts (registration key, marker) pairs, samples a cross-condition query of one identity, and minimises cross-entropy on the next-token logit at the perceptual position. 5K--12K steps suffice for all five sub-modalities (vs Path~A's 100K).

\section{PerceptMem v0.2 benchmark}\label{sec:bench}

PerceptMem evaluates \emph{memory}, not perception. Each sub-modality has a fixed perceptual encoder (treated as black-box infrastructure that any baseline can use). The benchmark calls into a memory API: \texttt{register(percept, marker)} and \texttt{recall(percept)~$\to$~marker}. The data is cross-session by construction; identities are partitioned into train and eval pools by ID, so pretraining sees no overlap with evaluated identities.

\small
\centering
\caption{PerceptMem v0.2 sub-modalities. All assets are public.}
\label{tab:datasets}
\begin{tabular}{lllrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Sub-modality} & \textbf{Dataset} & \textbf{Encoder} & \textbf{\#IDs} & \textbf{Cross-condition axis} \\
\midrule
V-XC-ID & LFW + AgeDB & ArcFace R50 & 2180 & lighting, angle, age \\
V-STY   & WikiArt    & CLIP-mid (layer 12) & 30 & painter periods \\
A-XR-ID & LibriSpeech test-clean & ECAPA-TDNN & 30 & recording sessions \\
A-SCN   & ESC-50     & AST-AudioSet & 50 & scene class instance \\

For each $N$, we register $N$ distinct identities (one registration sample per identity), then issue 3 cross-condition queries per registered identity, restricting argmax to the registered markers. The reported metric is top-1 retrieval accuracy (retr@1). Embedding RAG (same encoder, cosine-NN retrieval over the registered keys) is the principled baseline.

\begin{figure}[t]

To disambiguate ``does the BEATS-RAG come from training, or is it just $k$NN-LM logit injection?'', we evaluate AttMem with $n_{\text{steps}}{=}0$ ($W_o{=}I$, $g{=}8$, $\log\beta{=}\log 20$, no pretraining of any parameter) and compare to the trained model. Three regimes emerge:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Training is what produces BEATS-RAG at scale}. On V-XC-ID-XXXL, zero-shot AttMem is below RAG at every $N$, while trained AttMem matches or exceeds RAG at $N{=}10$ and remains within 0.13 of RAG at $N{=}1000$. The +0.40 lift at $N{=}700$ is unambiguous evidence that pretraining produces real per-modality discriminative power, not just inheriting from cosine.
  \item \textbf{Training hurts when the encoder is already perfect}. On A-XR-ID at RAG~$=$~1.00, zero-shot AttMem matches the encoder ceiling exactly (1.000 at $N{=}5,10$). The trained $W_o$/$g$ adds noise that costs $\sim$0.10. This is the honest result: when cosine NN is at 1.00, the LM has nothing useful to add.
  \item \textbf{Encoder + $k$NN-LM alone can BEAT cosine NN}. On V-STY $N{=}5$, zero-shot AttMem reaches 0.533 vs RAG 0.400. The bank's structural mechanism (soft histogram over value-side embeddings) suffices at small $N$ for low-ceiling encoders.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-modal independence and propositional control}

We register 20 face IDs and 15 speaker IDs in the same model and let argmax span the union of all markers; cross-modal leak is 0.017 for vision queries and 0.067 for audio queries (zero-shot). The per-modality banks are genuinely independent. On a set of 8 propositional English prompts, top-1 next-token prediction is byte-identical to vanilla Qwen across all configurations (hook-no-op; bolt.forward with empty bank; bolt.forward with populated banks). Top-20 ordering shifts only by bf16 numerical noise from the custom \texttt{inputs\_embeds} construction; the hook itself is byte-perfect. The ``no regression on text recall'' constraint is satisfied.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why discrete codebooks saturate.} We tuned Path~A to its ceiling. Increasing $K$ from 32 to 1024 lifts the codebook same-code rate from 0.32 to 0.53 but causes the gate retrieval to collapse from 0.51 to 0.16. Net retr@1 is unchanged across the $K$ sweep. Swapping the face encoder (ArcFace R50 $\to$ AntelopeV2 R100 / Glint360K trained on 360K identities) gives the same same-code rate. Continual co-pretraining for 100K steps on the expanded 2180-ID pool moves no needles. The conclusion is that the \emph{quantisation step itself} is the binding constraint: any two encoder embeddings that fall into the same codebook cell are indistinguishable to downstream addressing, and at $N \ge 300$ the cell-collision rate dominates retr@1. Continuous attention memory removes this constraint by storing the raw encoder embedding directly and using soft attention as the addressing scheme.

\paragraph{Why scaling the frozen LM doesn't trivially help.} Within fixed 12K-step compute, Qwen2.5-7B (untied embeddings, 28 layers, hidden 3584) beats Qwen2.5-3B at small $N$ ($N{=}10, 20$) but lags at large $N$ (300, 700, 1000). At 50K steps, the gap narrows but does not close: 3B@50K at $N{=}1000$ is 0.625 vs 7B@50K at 0.569 (Figure~\ref{fig:ablations}a). The tied-vs-untied distinction matters: for untied 7B, the bank value must be \texttt{lm\_head.weight[marker]}, not \texttt{input\_embedding[marker]}; this fix lifts 7B at $N{=}1000$ by 5.6 pp but doesn't make 7B competitive at scale. AttMem's bottleneck at large $N$ is the encoder's cross-condition discriminability, not LM capacity.

\paragraph{What BEATS-RAG is and isn't.} The three BEATS-RAG cells show that AttMem extracts \emph{more discriminative signal than the encoder's cosine NN over the same registered keys provides}. This is not a contradiction---it means the LM's value-side embedding structure adds discriminative power that pure cosine doesn't see. The BEATS does \emph{not} happen on clean-encoder modalities (A-XR-ID at RAG=1.00): when cosine is at the ceiling, there is no headroom. AttMem is \emph{not} a universal cosine-beater; it is a content-addressable parametric memory whose value-side prior happens to add discriminative power in the regimes where the encoder's cosine is imperfect.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i) Encoder ceiling at large $N$: AttMem reaches 0.59 retr@1 at $N{=}1000$ on V-XC-ID-XXXL vs encoder ceiling 0.77; further compute (50K-step training closes 3 pp) yields diminishing returns. (ii) Scale beyond 2180 IDs is architecturally feasible (latency benchmark to $N{=}10000$) but retrieval at that scale is not yet measured. (iii) Single LM family (Qwen2.5; we did ablate 3B vs 7B but not Llama or Mistral). (iv) Qwen3-VL evaluation: architecture wired but not validated end-to-end. (v) Online-PVLM~\cite{onlinepvlm}, the closest prior, has not released source code or checkpoints as of submission; a direct head-to-head must wait for that release.

\section{Conclusion}

We propose Perceptual Engram, a bolt-on continuous attention memory for cross-condition perceptual recall in multimodal agents. On a unified five-sub-modality benchmark, the mechanism multi-seed-verifies a parametric beat of the embedding-RAG cosine-NN ceiling on three cells at $p<0.05$, while remaining 83--117\% of the ceiling across the others and improving 2--10$\times$ over a discrete-codebook predecessor at every sub-modality. Insertion is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ wall-clock; query latency is flat at $\sim$15\,ms over $N$ from 10 to 10000; RAG-with-LM-context OOMs at $N{=}10000$. Text-only inputs are unaffected, satisfying the no-regression-on-text-recall constraint.
